PRE-REGISTRATION (written before anything was computed)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Calibration is reported with its before-and-after on BOTH the ML arm's absolute error AND the ML-minus-baseline gap, whichever direction each moves. Calibration may improve the ML arm's accuracy while shrinking its advantage over the homogeneous baseline; both numbers are reported and neither is selected after the fact."

In this milestone, 'absolute error' is read two ways and both are reported: (a) the one-step multiclass Brier score per origin
state, and (b) |predicted share - realised share| per class in percentage points. 'ML-minus-baseline gap' is the ML Brier minus the
homogeneous baseline's Brier. The baseline's one-step prediction for an origin state is its row of the transition matrix; fitted on
these same fit rows it IS the origin-conditional prior (checked against artifacts/baseline_headline/counts.csv in the report body). The 60-month
chained forecast is NOT rerun here; its absolute error and gap are the next milestone.

FOLDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
number of folds  : 5
seed             : 56
assignment method: by loan: fold = pandas.util.hash_array(loan_id, hash_key='0000000000000056') % 5 (keyed 64-bit siphash of the loan_id string; independent of row order and of the other loans)
each fold's model: trained on the other four folds' fit rows with the sidecar's own features, categories, lgb_params and 100 rounds
                   (identical configuration to the shipped model; only the training rows differ).
KNOWN AND ACCEPTED LIMITATION: folds are by loan, not by time, so a 2007 row may be predicted by a model trained on other loans' 2008
rows. This step measures calibration drift, not performance; by-loan folding is deliberate.

CALIBRATION METHODS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Four separate corrections, one per transient origin state (Current, DQ30, DQ60, DQ90+; DQ60 has its own), each fitted on that state's
out-of-fold rows, in two steps:
  STEP ONE  stay vs leave   : ISOTONIC regression of 1[to_state == origin] on the model's mass on the origin state.
            Used here because the cells are huge (Current: ~39M rows split roughly 98/2), so no bin is thin enough to chase noise.
  STEP TWO  destination split among leavers only: the model's non-origin mass renormalised to one, then PLATT (sigmoid) scaling,
            expit(a * logit(q) + b), fitted one-vs-rest per destination class and renormalised within step two.
            Used here because the cells are thin (Default is 0.0179% of pooled rows) and a staircase would fit noise and chain it sixty times.
  ASSEMBLE  row = [stay_prob on origin] + (1 - stay_prob) x [calibrated destination split]; sums to one by construction, asserted to
            1e-9 in float64 on every row, never renormalised afterwards.
The NAIVE scheme (comparison only): all six classes calibrated one-vs-rest on their own predicted probability, then the row renormalised.
The brief does not name its per-class method, so it is built both ways, isotonic per class and Platt per class; neither is preferred.
Numerics fixed in advance: logit() clips its argument to [1e-12, 1-1e-12]; the log-loss probability floor is 1e-15 (11 Oct decision);
a destination with under 100 leaver events at an origin is flagged THIN.
The correction is fitted on out-of-fold predictions and applied to the shipped model's IN-SAMPLE predictions on the same fit rows (the
measurement the brief specifies); the same measurement on the OOF predictions is also given, as a supplement.
No evaluate row is read.
